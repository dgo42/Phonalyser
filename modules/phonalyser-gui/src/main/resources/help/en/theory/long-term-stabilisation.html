Long-term average stabilisation

The question this page answers

A distortion measurement behind a twin-T notch filter can run for hours. The coherent average keeps adding phase-aligned blocks, and the harmonics rise out of a floor that falls as 1/√n. The block-to-block alignment is steered by the fundamental - the only line strong enough to steer by - and here the notch bites back: its phase at the fundamental drifts with the filter's temperature, and the analyser cannot tell a fundamental that moved on its own from a fundamental that moved because the whole block shifted in time. Read as a time shift, the notch's drift is applied to harmonic h with the factor h: a walk of one degree at the fundamental turns H2 by two degrees, H7 by seven and H9 by nine. Over a long run the harmonics smear and sink instead of rising.

The Fundamental phase alignment control exists for that. This page explains what the drift really looks like on a bench, and what its two modes read instead of the notched fundamental: Reference channel the tone on the channel that does not carry the notch, Pilot tone a second generated line read in the same block as the fundamental.

Why one degree matters. DAC pre-distortion cancels a harmonic by adding its anti-phase twin from the generator. A cancellation 30 dB deep leaves a residual of 3 % of the raw harmonic. A correction computed while the fundamental's phase was off by an angle e is applied h·e off at harmonic h, and the residual it leaves is raw × |1 − ej·h·e| ≈ raw × h·e. For 40 dB of cancellation at H7 the fundamental's phase has to be right to 0.08° while the correction is being measured. That is the accuracy the stabilisation has to deliver, and the reason a sweep every ten minutes cannot be the whole answer.

What drifts, and by how much

Across its null a twin-T notch turns the phase by about 180 degrees, and the phase falls with rising frequency: on one reference filter it goes from about +135° a hertz below the null to about −45° a hertz above it, with a slope of 80 to 100 degrees per hertz half a hertz from the null and about 50 degrees per hertz further out. When the filter warms, its null moves down in frequency; when it cools, the null moves up and deepens (about 4.6 dB per kelvin on that filter). For a fundamental held at a fixed frequency this means: the phase at the fundamental rises when the filter cools, because the null moves up and away, and falls when it warms.

The numbers on that bench, with the fundamental half a hertz below the null: −28 degrees per kelvin of enclosure temperature, drift rates up to 0.2-0.4 degrees per minute during an ordinary room-temperature swing, and over ten-minute stretches a phase change of more than one degree in a quarter of all cases, up to eight degrees. The fundamental's level after the notch moves with the phase - about 0.25 to 0.3 dB per degree on the flank - because both are one and the same quantity, the position of the null relative to the fundamental.

Where to put the fundamental. On a flank, not in the bottom of the null. In the bottom the level after the notch is lowest and least stable, and the level's slope is zero, so a shift of the null in either direction reads the same. On a flank the level is a clean, one-to-one reading of the null's position. A fundamental of about −82 dBV after the notch - a few hundred microvolts from a 1 V tone - is a good working point. The tune-notch measurement is a tool for tuning the filter itself; which side of the null the measurement frequency sits on, and how far from it, is the user's choice - the DAC pre-distortion wizard selects no frequency either.

What is in this version

The Reference channel mode reads the excess on every block from the second ADC channel, which carries the tone ahead of the notch. The Pilot tone mode reads it on every block from a second generated line at an odd multiple of half the fundamental, whose phase is measured in the same block as the fundamental's.

Bottom line

  • The enemy is the notch's phase at the fundamental, which drifts by tens of degrees per kelvin and is applied to harmonic h with the factor h; the pre-distortion needs it right to a tenth of a degree.
  • Put the fundamental on a flank, about −82 dBV after the notch, never in the null's bottom.
